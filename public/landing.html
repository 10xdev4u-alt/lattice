Why the AI tool you used to write your paper can't help you defend it.

Every research AI you've tried — Elicit, Consensus, Scite, ChatGPT with ScholarGPT — gives you a polished answer or a table. None of them show you how they got there. When your committee asks "why this paper, why this quote, why this comparison," you have nothing to show.

Lattice flips it. The page is the audit log. Every claim, every tool call, every source is reviewable, replayable, citable. You can export the trail as a Markdown methods appendix and drop it into your thesis.

14 typed tools, not a chat box

Every Lattice tool has a name, a description, a JSON-Schema input, and an annotation telling the agent whether it's read-only or write. The agent can't guess what to do. It can only call what you've declared.

The page IS the audit log

Open the Log tab. See every tool call, every arg, every result, every duration. Click a step. Replay it. Export it as a PRISMA-style methods appendix. This is the WebMCP #261 feature — reviewable workflow documents.

Two agents, one page

The peer-reviewer persona is the killer demo. Your primary agent drafts a review. A second agent — the skeptic — challenges every claim, demands citations, never writes to the document. You watch them negotiate.

PDF and LaTeX, not just abstracts

Drop a PDF, or paste an arXiv ID. The two-column read order is reconstructed. The per-paper search index is built. The text is yours — not a vendor's.

Bibliographies in five formats

CSL-JSON (the lingua franca), BibTeX, RIS, APA-Markdown, MLA-Markdown. Round-trips through Zotero, JabRef, and Pandoc.
